## Candidate: typora `app.asar` is a 3-member bytecode loader — 99.4% of bytes are a single `.jsc` V8 code-cache blob

- claim: archive members are exactly `package.json` (251 B), `launch.dist.js` (1,383 B), `atom.compiled.dist.jsc` (388,080 B — 99.42% of archive bytes; sha256 `6a0cb7b32270498ed190c589ca29c990a0e4b5f06f0cd6a5516e19a715c2dfe9`). `package.json` declares `name: Typora, version: 1.14.9, main: launch.dist.js`. The extracted `launch.dist.js` (full 1,383 B read) is a bytenode-pattern loader: `v8.setFlagsFromString("--no-lazy")`, `v8.setFlagsFromString("--no-flush-bytecode")`, then `Module._extensions[".jsc"]=Module._extensions[".cjsc"]=function(e,t){…}` — a handler that `fs.readFileSync`s the .jsc, patches the flag-hash header (`dummyBytecode.slice(12,16).copy(e,12)`), builds `new vm.Script(o,{filename:t,…,cachedData:a})`, throws on `cachedDataRejected`, `runInThisContext`s it, and `.apply`s the returned function over `[exports, require, module, filename, dirname, process, global]` (the standard CommonJS wrapper). Last statement: `require("./atom.compiled.dist.jsc")`. The real Typora application logic lives entirely in V8 bytecode; the only static JS is the loader.
- source: `asar list` + `extract-file` (run inside /tmp per r1's cwd-materialization warning); `cat package.json`; full `launch.dist.js` read; `sha256sum` on extracted `.jsc`.
- verify: `cd /tmp && npx -y @electron/asar extract-file /usr/share/typora/resources/app.asar launch.dist.js && cat launch.dist.js` — loader text verbatim above.
- verified-at: 2026-10-13
- suggested: admit — decisive app-shape fact; any "Typora security surface" claim from JS statics must be scoped to this loader only.

## Summary

The census widened exactly as round 2 hoped and surfaced an app shape obsidian never showed. Enumeration now covers `/usr/share/typora` (3 archives — the r1 sweep's root list missed them entirely), signal-desktop (38.5 MB + unpacked tree), and 1Password (40.7 MB + unpacked), all sha256-pinned. The smallest unanalyzed target, typora `app.asar` (390,514 B), turns out to be 99.4% a single V8 bytecode blob: `launch.dist.js` is a 1,383-byte bytenode-pattern loader (`v8.setFlagsFromString("--no-lazy")`, `Module._extensions[".jsc"]` handler, `vm.Script` with `cachedData`, `.apply` over the CJS wrapper) that `require`s `atom.compiled.dist.jsc` (388,080 B). `rea-agents@6.4.0 analyze-javascript-application` completes cleanly (RC=0, `ev_184ef0ac…`, 475,737 B Evidence, artifact sha256 pinned) and faithfully models exactly what is parseable: a 20-node application graph whose only source-module is the loader, 12 unresolved-reference nodes (including the dynamic `e.require(t)` and the `.jsc` specifier anchored at line 1 col 1345–1380), a 268-node semantic graph with 84 typed unknowns, and an all-zero IPC/Electron-boundary summary. The structural comparison against obsidian's r2 run is the round's payload: identical envelope, taxonomy, relation vocabulary, unknowns families, and summary-view paging — but obsidian's zeroes were informative (its parsed main.js genuinely registers no channels) while typora's identical zeroes cover only 0.42% of archive bytes; the `.jsc` gets no graph node at all and `parse_failures: 0` is an inventory exclusion, not a parse. `analyze-javascript-application` sees the door and cannot see the room — a bytecode lane is the named frontier for typora, and signal/1Password remain open at ~100× size needing raised `--max-heap-mb` and `--filter-output` discipline. Recorded honestly: one operator jq `-n` error (recovered), four archives deliberately deferred.

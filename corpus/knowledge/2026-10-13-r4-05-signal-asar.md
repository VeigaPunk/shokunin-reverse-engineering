## Summary

Signal Desktop's 38.5 MB `app.asar` (1,866 members, Signal 8.26.0) is the largest ASAR on the host and could not be analyzed whole: three distinct, honestly-typed walls bound it. **(1) Integrity:** default `integrity_policy: fail` refuses the archive outright — all four unpacked `.node` natives (libsignal-client, ringrtc, sqlcipher, fs-xattr) contradict their declared ASAR hashes because the distro post-processed them; `record-and-continue` records each as `observed-untrusted` and proceeds. **(2) Per-source timeout:** the 300 s default is per-file but expiry aborts the *entire* run with a typed `provider_timeout` + partial Evidence; even at 900 s/source the 1200 s envelope ends in `cancelled` with a 62 MB partial (10/1,008 files). **(3) Worker heap:** the `--max-heap-mb 8192` worker OOMs during semantic projection on *small* webpack chunks (68–92 KB files, dense minified CJS object graphs — RSS observed at 7.8 GB on one 89 KB file), so the pathological inputs are chunks, not the 6 MB preload. The Pareto-admissible route-around: a `--artifact-format directory` pass over the 9.2 MB security core (`package.json` + HTML + `bundles/` minus `chunks/`) **completes RC=0 in ~13 min** (`ev_f9b4493a…`, 19/19 parsed) and yields Signal's full privileged surface: 173 IPC operations (101 main handlers, `handle`-dominated, `sql-channel:*` family to SQLCipher), 95 literal channels verified 1:1 against grep, 8 hardened BrowserWindows (`nodeIntegration:!1` everywhere; 7 fully sandboxed; the lone `sandbox:!1` main window runs the entire 6 MB app as an unsandboxed preload behind a script-less strict-CSP `background.html`), 13 contextBridge APIs, 4 custom `protocol.handle` schemes, deny-by-default navigation/webview/permission handlers — and **zero sender validations**, the one weak axis. Versus obsidian (r3-10): same envelope/taxonomy/walls, but Signal is ~2.1× the IPC surface, inverts the privilege posture (isolated vs fully-privileged renderers), ships an integrity-contradicted native set, and is ~30× less tractable to whole-archive analysis — the open frontier is chunked `directory` passes per subtree, not a bigger single knob (heap ceiling is 16 GiB and chunks already exceed 8).

## 3. The design decision that makes this a *drift* gate

Three rounds of pointer bites (`CR5.md:144-169` round 1; `SCORECARD.md:68-72`
round 2) share one shape: **the citing sentence states a line number and the
number is wrong.** A gate that regex-pins the *citing line's own text* passes a
sentence stating a wrong number, because the sentence is unchanged — only its
referent moved. So the primary entry kind is `extracted`: it reads the target
line number **out of** the citing line and verifies the *named target* actually
holds the content the sentence claims. A reword that states the right number
passes; any number it states wrong fails. This is what makes the gate catch the
class rather than the instance.

Entry kinds: `extracted` (read the number, check the target), `exact` (the citing
line itself), `anchor` (content must appear, and a hit on a line other than the
pinned one is `citation_moved`). A `stamp` field marks a pointer qualified by a
capture stamp — it is then recorded as `citation_historical_ok` and does **not**
redden the run, because the entry's own text names the revision it was accurate
at. An `exempt` list records tokens deliberately not checked, each with its
cause, so the omission is auditable rather than silent.

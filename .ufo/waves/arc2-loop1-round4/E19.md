### 5.3 Per-process accounting at kill (kernel `Tasks state`, my arithmetic)

The kernel dump lists every task in the scope (pages × 4096 B; my computation):

| process | rss pages | GiB | swapents (pages) |
|---|---|---|---|
| java (victim, 4181543) | 839,710 | 3.203 | 234,002 (= 914 MiB) |
| 10 × `decompile` workers | 954,961 combined | 3.643 | 0 |
| node (4177041) | 34,999 | 0.134 | 24,622 |
| npm (4175743) | 8,609 | 0.033 | 3,356 |
| **scope total** | **1,838,279** | **7.012** | — |

This is the sharpest reading of the death: the JVM alone (3.20 GiB) was *not* over
the cap — the **scope** was, because the ten native decompiler workers together
held more than the JVM (3.64 GiB) and both were being pushed into the saturated
1 GiB swap. The JVM's swapents (914 MiB) also show it was already partially swapped
out when killed. The OOM was triggered by a `decompile` worker faulting in a file
page (`filemap_fault` in the call trace, `try_charge_memcg` → `mem_cgroup_out_of_memory`),
but the kernel selected the JVM (highest score in-scope) as victim.

## 7. Conclusions, labeled

**Observation (commands above):**
- The run was alive at 19:04:38Z (sample 1), dead by 19:07:45Z (systemd+kernel),
  and its scope/scratch were already removed when samples 2 and 3 ran.
- Death cause: memcg OOM at the 7 GiB `MemoryMax` (kernel `usage == limit ==
  7,340,032 kB`, failcnt 5,359; swap at 99.998 % with 53,192 charge failures).
- Exactly one process died (the in-scope JVM); zero global OOMs in the window.
- REA reports only a typed `cancelled` envelope; no OOM in its output.
- At kill the scope's RSS was 7.012 GiB: workers 3.643 GiB > JVM 3.203 GiB
  (+914 MiB of the JVM in swap) > node 0.137 + npm 0.034 GiB.

**Inference (from the above):**
- On this configuration the cgroup cap is the *first* wall: the run reached
  `MemoryMax` in 4 m 42 s, so neither the 7200 s REA deadline nor any deeper
  analysis phase could be exercised. A decisive run needs either a larger cap
  (with the CR2 caveats about host-level OOM risk) or a smaller working set
  (fewer decompiler workers / smaller target), not more time.
- The round-3 picture ("the constraint moved to wall-clock depth", `LOG.md:1091`)
  holds only *without* a cgroup cap: the same target under `-Xmx8G` uncontained
  survived 30 m 01 s (outer-cap kill, `LOG.md:1088`), while the contained 6G/7G
  variant died at 4 m 42 s. Containment changed the binding constraint back to
  memory — a trade the recipe makes deliberately, and it worked as designed
  (kill stayed in-scope).

**Unknown:**
- Whether a re-run with the same knobs but, e.g., 12 GiB `MemoryMax` would have
  reached a typed deadline/refusal or completed — not tested this session.
- Whether the worker-count burst (10 decompile children at kill) is
  deterministic or load-dependent — one death, no repeat.

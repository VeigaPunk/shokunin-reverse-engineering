## Findings a reader must carry into scoring
- The strongest single limit is **L16**: the frozen label's `ownrec 1 of 1` is a formatted literal
  and the L1's own green receipt on disk contains no `ownrec` field at all. A reader who takes
  `ownrec 1 of 1` as evidence that the rule re-read and verified its receipt is reading a
  constant, not a measurement. (`kill-rule-v3.py:735-739`, `:782-784`.)
- **L1 + L2 + L5** together mean the receipt's byte binding is a *content* check on a *file*, and
  the rule's own re-read is a *content* check on a *file*; neither binds a verdict to the process
  that produced it. The dirty-clone leg (L11) closes the round-96 M96-a lie only for the one
  scenario it constructs, in the one clone topology it constructs.
- **L22** means the C2 depth instrument's discrimination against round-91's v3 is demonstrated
  only on an inline synthetic decoy; the real block 36 is accepted by both, so the C2 claim's
  "strengthened instrument" is real but its *effect on the landed block* is not measured.
- **L13** means `mutants killed 21 of 21` includes at least one row whose "crash" could have been
  an anchor-drift `ValueError` rather than the intended uncaught exception. All 21 `find`
  anchors were checked to occur exactly once in the landing bytes, so this is a latent risk.
- **L25** is the one limit that would have turned the round-97 rule green from a non-root cwd
  had the leg done what its docstring says. The rule's own `--mutants` and `--fixtures` are also
  **relative**, never joined with `--root` (`kill-rule-v3.py:604, 643, 649`), so command A only
  runs correctly from the lane root — the M96-j class the K-cwd leg was written to close is still
  open in the rule, and the leg that should catch it never moves the cwd it names.

## Conclusions (labeled)

- **Observation:** the same four E-family IDs, E25–E28, are absent from all three 60-report archives for R2–R4; R1 has all 64. R2–R4 each used Devin. R1's completed E25 was in the initial 18; completed E26–E28 were in the 46 Devin replacements.
- **Observation:** R1's documented E25–E28 work spans census, REA decode, custody capture, and hygiene. It does not show a single shared long-context scope shape.
- **Inference:** the recurring missing set is strong evidence of a stable seat/dispatch-pattern defect (4/64, three consecutive rounds), and a Devin interaction is a plausible hypothesis because R2–R4 are homogeneous Devin dispatches. However, R1's successful Devin replacements among E26–E28 rule out “Devin always drops these executor IDs” as a sufficient explanation. With only one mixed-provider successful round and no R2–R4 per-seat telemetry, provider causality is unproven.
- **Unknown:** what scopes E25–E28 received in R2–R4, whether those scopes were long, whether a dispatch call failed versus work started and timed out, and whether the repeated IDs reflect ordering, payload size, retry bookkeeping, provider behavior, or another shared cause.

## 2. Strongest defence of the depth label (and why it is not enough)

Two independent defences can be mounted.

**Defence A (surface-based):** AXES.md defines depth as "verifiable technical detail — decision tables with real thresholds, named error signatures, cited sources". The gate ships a verdict table (CLEAN/REFUSED/UNEVALUABLE → 0/2/3), real thresholds (`TARGET`, `MIN_SHORTCUTS = 3` at `replay-gate.py:31`, `TIMEOUT = 300` at `:32`), and named error signatures (`config_shortcut_count`, `config_shortcut_duplicate_class`, `counter_receipt_unreadable`, `counter_receipt_fields`, `counter_target_mismatch`, `positive_control_absent`, `shortcut_absent:<class>`). If those landed as a SKILL/corpus table, depth would move.

*Why it fails:* they did not land at the measured revision — zero SKILL/corpus hits at `c79bbe7b`, zero at tip. The depth *surface* is definitionally the SKILL/corpus surface ("A reader can act on SKILL.md alone"); an instrument that carries a decision table internally is not that surface. This is the same reading the lane itself uses: the depth proxy counts exactly those rule blocks that live in the craft section of SKILL.md and carry resolvable corpus citations. The gate has no SKILL row, no knowledge entry, no citation; its only "source" is the obligation label `QUALITY 67` (`replay-gate.py:4`), a lesson pointer, not a cited source. So a reader of SKILL.md at the measured revision cannot act on the gate at all — the depth signal is literally absent. Defence A collapses on the measured surface.

**Defence B (capability-fit):** AXES.md explicitly allows a claim to be a capability acquisition and still requires a per-axis verdict (`AXES.md:40-41`). One could argue the label should be the axis the *capability services*: this gate is metrology for structural-decoding counters, i.e. it equips the depth-measurement process itself, so "depth" is the axis it serves. Lane precedent exists for owned-axis labels on capabilities: round 17 recorded "C2 depth + capability" (`SCORECARD.md:2370`) and round 14 recorded "C2 calibration / capability" (`SCORECARD.md:2196`). So "a capability can carry an owned-axis label" is true.

*Why it fails:* when the lane attached an owned-axis label to a capability, the capability's **content** was that axis's subject matter — round-17's depth capability was a retained run-bound carrier/identity discrimination on real Go binaries with a knowledge entry and source rows (S081), i.e. genuine depth content; round-14's calibration capability was a quote-regression audit, i.e. genuine calibration content. R34-C2's capability content is a generic gate that runs any counter argv and reads its self-declared receipt fields against a synthetic 8-fixture corpus; its *purpose* (refuse a freeze when a memorizing reader meets the target) is the calibration definition verbatim — "overreach caught" (`AXES.md:15`). The lane's default for instrument-shaped capabilities is exactly this: the four rows CritAxisFit cited are all labeled "operability (fleet axis, unowned proxy; capability acquisition)" (`SCORECARD.md:688,750,913,1207`), where the capability content is a battery/custody/probe — instrument plumbing. And when a capability was labeled on an *owned* axis, it carried that axis's subject-matter content — which this one does not.

So the corrected precedent is: owned-axis label requires owned-axis content; otherwise the instrument-shaped capability is operability (fleet) — or, at best for this claim, calibration (owned), because the gate is a self-assessment instrument whose subject matter is counter overreach. Under *either* reading, the depth label is the least-supported choice. The label is not merely "unsupported"; it is contradicted both by the absent surface (A) and by the lane's own labeling convention (B).

## 3. Is the stop moot? No — but the verdict overstates what is at stake

The stop record itself (claim-stop.json) is independent of the axis question: the stop rests on the decisive counterexample (nameset.py 8/8 on the green surface, reproduced here in §1) and the kill rule ("a target met by a control that skips the claimed work is not an improvement"). That stop would stand even if the axis field were `operability` or `calibration`. So:

- **For the round's admission/score:** the question is moot. No capability row enters; no score moves (`not_claimed` list, `scores 10 -> 10`); the round is stopped. Nothing about the axis verdict changes that.
- **For the record and precedent:** the question is *not* moot. `claims.json` (frozen) and claim-stop.json both keep `axis: depth` as the declared shape of the round. That is recorded history and should not be rewritten — but if the round report were to say "depth was worked", that would be a false self-assessment (a calibration defect), and the precedent would survive for future rounds: a later claim shaped like this one would be free to re-declare depth and point at R34 as precedent. The wave-1 verdict is therefore a real *record* finding, not a scoring claim. That is the right weight for it.

CritAxisFit's §3.2 goes one step too far, though: it says the depth label is "what makes this round satisfy the lowest-axis obligation on paper". That is not the strongest defence, and it is also not the only structure at stake. The three owned proxies are tied at 10.0 (coverage 10.0, depth 10.0, calibration 10.0; `SCORECARD.md:23-25`) with none retired, so **any** owned-axis label (coverage/depth/calibration) would satisfy `AXES.md:32-34`; indeed round 24 worked coverage and round 33/34 worked depth under the same tie. The card itself records "depth is tied-lowest without a stagnation waiver" (`SCORECARD.md:3327`), so the depth label does have the *declared* obligation behind it — but it is not the *only* label that could carry it. The real harm of the mislabel is therefore precedent formation and the false "depth worked" self-assessment, not obligation-gaming.

## 4. Verdict on the verdict

1. The verdict's evidentiary core re-derives cleanly: surface absent at the measured revision (and at tip); proxy 17/17 → 17/17; `predicate_identity` false (A 237 vs B 351 chars); `gate_present` tautological; the decisive counterexample reproduces 8/8 on three seeds.
2. The conclusion that the moved surface is **not depth** is correct. The strongest attack on the depth label (Defence A) fails because the depth surface is the SKILL/corpus surface and it is empty of gate content at the measured revision; the strongest alternative defence (Defence B) fails because the lane's own labeling convention ties owned-axis labels to that axis's subject-matter content, which a generic admission harness does not carry. The best-fitting labels are operability (fleet, unowned) or calibration (owned — self-assessment instrument; the r14 "calibration / capability" precedent).
3. The verdict's secondary claim that the depth label is a prerequisite for the lowest-axis obligation is overstated: any tied-lowest owned axis label would satisfy it. The residual harm is in the record/precedent, not in obligation-gaming; the stop itself is independent of the axis field and stands.

No score overclaim, no target/counter relaxation; the label is the sole defect (a calibration/record defect), and the smallest safe correction is to record the claim's per-axis verdict as no-owned-axis-moved (capability row only, if and when the L1 lands one), without letting the declared `depth` field imply a worked depth surface.

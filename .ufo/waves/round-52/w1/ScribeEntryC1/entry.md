## The device-accounting law

[observation] **The law, as this round states it (the claim text; the rule encodes it, not a detail whitelist):** the extraction producer (REA 6.5.0 `extract-firmware`, handler `squashfs_v4_le`, unblob 26.6.4 → sasquatch `-le`) materialises regular files and directories and re-creates symlinks (with a `MaliciousSymlinkRemoved` diagnostic when it refuses one), but it creates **no device nodes** — an unprivileged extraction cannot `mknod` a device node — and it emits **no gap record for device rows**: a char-device/block-device oracle entry carries **no disposition at all**. Because the law is that no gap record exists for such an entry, the frozen rule admits **no** gap kind for those kinds (`allowed` for a device kind is the empty list; `required['char-device'] = false`), so a gap landing on a device row is a **violation** of the law, not a tolerated state: a future extractor that starts emitting a device gap turns the rule **red** (fail closed). The round-52 law rule draft reads exactly this way (`.ufo/waves/round-52/w1/ExecRuleFixtures/gap-rule.json`): `allowed` for `char-device`, `block-device`, `fifo` and `socket` is `[]`; `required.char-device` is `false`; `removed.silent` is `{}`; `claim` is `R52-C1`.

## Limits (the limits of R52-C1, restated)

1. [observation] **Vendor bytes are fetched, never committed.** AVM's licence permits one personal backup copy only (S249). An image AVM replaces makes the chain unfetchable (the four are long-standing releases of their models, not a guarantee), and the counter-time rerun is unrunnable without a `--carrier-cache`. The committed receipts and oracle tables are the durable evidence.
2. [observation] **The extraction tree is never committed.** REA's extraction output lives under the scratch carrier cache and is not part of the lane; what is committed is the receipt (the envelope, the gaps, the diagnostics) and the frozen oracle / non-regular tables. The receipt is the only record of the tree.
3. [observation] **Availability is not holding.** The claim's subject is four pinned vendor images the publisher may withdraw or replace; the round-52 data run's reproducibility depends on the chain being fetchable at that moment. Nothing in this entry should be read as a statement that the four images will remain available.
4. [observation] The AVM marker is invisible to unblob and re-stampable: unblob 26.6.4 tests `mkfs_time == bytes_used` only inside its **big-endian** v4 branch (S245 :48), so an AVM-marker LE member and a standard LE member take one handler and one argv.
